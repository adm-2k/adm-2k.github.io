Work

What I've built, taught, researched, and designed. Nearly all of it with other people — I've tried to mark my own part honestly. The one-page version is on the résumé page.

Software

Participant-management platform — The Upskilling Labs

Next.js · 2026– · in production

The Upskilling Labs has run thirty-five-odd events so far this year — workshops, hackathons, summits — reaching about five hundred people. I help build and maintain the platform the programs run on: registration and application intake, milestone tracking, feedback collection, and user management. Most weeks that means content updates, small fixes, and support, shared with the rest of the team.

CIB Mango Tree — Civic Tech DC

Python, NLP · open source · 2025–

A tool for detecting coordinated inauthentic behavior in social-media datasets, developed in public by volunteers at Civic Tech DC. I’m one contributor among several; so far that has meant tokenizer improvements, edits to the documentation, some video work, and a whitepaper proposing multilingual support that is still in draft. With the organization’s director I am also sketching a Civic Tech Academy, an educational program meant to move skills between project teams.

Media & operations

Production and operations — Storyboard Studios

Premiere Pro, Python, Bash · contract · 2025–

Ongoing contract work for a documentary studio. It began in pre-production: I wrote scripts that automated media intake — ingestion, tagging, download handling — cutting intake time by roughly forty percent, and kept records consistent across two concurrent projects. This summer the work has been video editing for a sizzle reel shown at a Black Economic Forum event at Martha’s Vineyard, organizing documents and spreadsheets for the United Justice Coalition conference, and design and administrative tasks as they come up.

Teaching & organizing

GitHub for Team Collaboration

Curriculum & instruction · recurring

A workshop series I lead at The Upskilling Labs on version control and collaborative development: branching, issues, pull requests, review. It assumes no programming background and runs on real repositories rather than slides. The materials are public: workshop-template and GitHub-2.0-Workshop.

Programming & events — The Upskilling Labs

Working board · 2026–

With my co-lead I run the workshop program end to end: scheduling and logistics, mentor and partner outreach, a three-month curriculum we wrote together, and the memos and documentation that keep facilitators and participants oriented across this year’s thirty-five events.

Computational social science

Public-health discourse — Columbia Health Language Lab

CDC-funded · Python, NLP · 2021–2023

As a research assistant on a CDC-funded project I compiled and analyzed a corpus of more than a hundred thousand documents, tracking shifts in public discourse around health. I also kept the team’s shared datasets and documentation in order and presented findings, with original visualizations, to faculty in several departments.

The language of party platforms — Laidlaw Fellowship

WZB Manifesto Project corpus · Python · 2020–2021

A two-summer fellowship measuring how five German political parties differentiate themselves in language, using the WZB Manifesto Project corpus. I wrote the proposal, ran the analysis in Python, and presented the results at an undergraduate research fair.

Humanities

Collecting and modernism — with Dr. Annie Pfeifer

Research assistance · German studies · 2021–2023

For To the Collector Belong the Spoils: Modernism and the Art of Appropriation, Dr. Pfeifer’s study of modernist writers as collectors, hoarders, and appropriators, I tracked down sources and compiled research — Walter Benjamin above all. My name is in the foreword.

Fordism in interwar Germany

In progress · begun at the Freie Universität Berlin

A project that started in a Berlin pro-seminar: how Fordism arrived in Germany between the wars and what it did to labor relations and political discourse. I’m picking it back up this year.

Design

Product design — Columbia Daily Spectator

Section lead · 2020–2023

Three years on the product team of Columbia’s independent student newspaper, the last as Product Design Section Lead. I ran a cross-functional group of twelve designers and sixteen developers, built the training program for ten incoming designers, and sat between design and engineering during the platform’s React migration. The section’s tools — The Shaft for housing, LionClubs for student organizations, CULPA for course reviews — served over three hundred thousand readers a year.

Earlier

A year in Tübingen on a State Department CBYX scholarship, including an internship in the Bundestag with the SPD. Several years of freelance photography: thirty-odd portrait and product sessions a year, plus event coverage for Columbia and the Smithsonian Channel. Some of the photographs are here.
