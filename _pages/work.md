## Building

### Participant-management platform — The Upskilling Labs
<p class="meta">Next.js · registration, milestones, feedback · 2026–present · in production</p>

[The Upskilling Labs](https://theupskillinglabs.org) runs twice-weekly technology
and project-management workshops for professionals building new skills — 30+
sessions and 500+ participants per program cycle. I build and maintain the web
platform behind it: registration and application intake, milestone tracking,
feedback collection, and content and user management across the participant
lifecycle. It's unglamorous, load-bearing software — the kind a small nonprofit
actually runs on.

### CIB Mango Tree — Civic Tech DC
<p class="meta">Open source · Python, NLP · contributor &amp; documentation · 2025–present</p>

An open-source tool built in public by volunteers at
[Civic Tech DC](https://civictechdc.org), where I work through the project's
normal open-source process: issues, pull requests, and code review. My
contributions so far: improvements to the **NLP tokenizer**, writing and
maintaining the **project documentation**, and a **whitepaper proposing
multilingual support**. I'm also partnering with the organization's director to
design a *Civic Tech Academy* — a cross-team, open-source educational initiative
for inter-team skill building.

### Media-intake automation — Storyboard Studios
<p class="meta">Python, Bash · documentary post-production · 2025</p>

During pre-production on a documentary, I automated the studio's media-intake
pipeline — scripted ingestion, tagging, and download handling that cut
data-entry and transfer time by roughly **40%** — while keeping footage,
transcripts, and research retrievable across two concurrent projects' tracking
systems.

## Teaching & organizing

### GitHub for Team Collaboration
<p class="meta">Curriculum + live instruction · recurring series · materials on GitHub</p>

A recurring workshop series I lead at The Upskilling Labs, teaching version
control and collaborative Git/GitHub workflows — branches, issues, pull
requests, review — to non-technical professionals. The materials are open:
[workshop-template](https://github.com/adm-2k/workshop-template) and
[GitHub-2.0-Workshop](https://github.com/adm-2k/GitHub-2.0-Workshop).
Watching someone merge their first pull request never gets old.

## Research

### Public-health discourse at scale — Columbia Health Language Lab
<p class="meta">CDC-funded · 100,000+ document corpus · Python, NLP · 2021–2023</p>

As a research assistant on a CDC-funded project, I retrieved and analyzed a
corpus of **100,000+ text documents** using computational methods to measure
shifts in public discourse, maintained the team's shared datasets and
documentation systems, and presented findings — with original visualizations —
to faculty across multiple departments.

### The language of party platforms — Laidlaw Fellowship
<p class="meta">WZB Manifesto Project corpus · Python · 2020–2021</p>

A two-summer research fellowship quantifying how five German political parties
differentiate themselves linguistically, using the WZB Manifesto Project
dataset. I designed the proposal, ran a multi-method analysis in Python, and
presented the findings with original visualizations at an undergraduate
research fair.

## Design

### Product design — Columbia Daily Spectator
<p class="meta">Section Lead · 12 designers, 16 developers · 300,000+ annual readers · 2020–2023</p>

Three years on the product team of Columbia's independent student newspaper,
ending as Product Design Section Lead. I led a cross-functional team of 12
designers and 16 developers, built training modules and onboarded 10 incoming
trainees, and served as the design–engineering liaison during the platform's
React migration. Our tools served **300,000+ unique readers a year**:
[The Shaft](https://theshaft.info/) (housing),
[LionClubs](https://www.columbiaspectator.com/spectrum/2021/06/08/lionclubs-columbias-new-go-to-streamlined-club-database/)
(student organizations), and [CULPA](https://culpa.info/) (course and professor
reviews).
